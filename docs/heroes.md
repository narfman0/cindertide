# Cindertide — Heroes

---

## Overview

Heroes are larger-than-life commanders fielded alongside your army. They are recruited like units but persist across missions, accumulate experience, and carry visual evidence of what they've survived. Players control all units including heroes directly on the battlefield.

---

## Roster

A small fixed roster of 5 heroes total across the campaign. You begin with a choice of 2; others are recruited through campaign events and story beats. You field up to 2 heroes per mission.

Heroes do not count toward population cap.

---

## Progression

Heroes gain experience from combat within a single mission. Tiers reset at the start of each mission — XP does not carry across campaign. This is intentional: cross-mission persistence would incentivize farming earlier missions for free power before harder ones.

Experience unlocks predefined upgrade tiers — no player choice, no skill tree. Each tier makes the hero meaningfully stronger and changes their appearance.

### Tier Thresholds
| Tier | Name | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Raw | Starting state |
| 2 | Seasoned | First visual change, aura radius increases |
| 3 | Veteran | Signature ability strengthens, new visual element added |
| 4 | Legend | Full visual transformation, aura effect expands, ability reaches final form |

Visual changes accumulate — scars, improvised armor, burns, modifications. A Tier 4 hero looks like they've been through a war because they have.

---

## Hero Mechanics

### Aura
Each hero has a passive aura affecting nearby friendly units. Aura radius increases with tier. The aura is always on — no activation required.

### Signature Ability
One active ability per hero. Not a cooldown to spam — it builds a charge meter through combat presence (dealing damage, taking fire, holding ground). When fully charged, it can be released once for a decisive, visually dominant effect. Recharge begins immediately after use.

### Going Down
Heroes do not die — they go down. When a hero reaches 0 HP:
- They collapse and enter a **downed state** for 30 seconds
- Any friendly unit can reach them to **recover** them at 25% HP
- If not recovered in time, they are **incapacitated** for the rest of the mission
- An incapacitated hero misses the next mission as well (recovering)
- If a hero is incapacitated 3 times total across the campaign, they carry a permanent minor debuff (visual scar + small stat penalty) — they're diminished but not gone

## The Roster

### 1. Marta Voss — The Ironhand
**Background:** Former Combine engineer who lost her right arm to one of the first robot uprisings. Replaced it with a salvaged robot limb she reverse-engineered herself. Neither fully trusts the Ironborn nor fully fears them.

**Aura — Repair Instinct:** Nearby vehicles and robots passively regenerate a small amount of HP over time.

**Signature Ability — Overclock:** Voss overloads the machine limb, sending a pulse through nearby mechanical units — vehicles and robots gain bonus speed and attack rate for 12 seconds. Visually: orange-gold energy crawls across every machine near her.

**Tier progression:** Limb gets more elaborate modifications. By Tier 4 the arm extends partway up her shoulder, glowing faintly at rest.

**Role:** Economy/sustain hero. Keep her near your vehicle line and she keeps it fighting longer.

---

### 2. Dagan Rel — The Ashwalker
**Background:** Covenant deserter. Walked out of a battlefield into a ruin that shouldn't have been there and walked out the other side changed — he doesn't talk about what he saw. Sees things others don't. The ancient factions behave oddly around him — not hostile, just *aware*.

**Aura — Dread Presence:** Nearby enemies have reduced morale recovery rate. Enemy units near Rel break faster and rout sooner.

**Signature Ability — Stillpoint:** Rel goes motionless and the world around him slows — enemy units in a wide radius move and fire at half speed for 8 seconds. He cannot move or act during this. Visually: desaturation bleeds outward from him, color draining from the affected zone.

**Tier progression:** Eyes become slightly wrong — too still. By Tier 4 a faint afterimage follows him when he moves.

**Role:** Disruption/morale hero. Devastating in large engagements, fragile if isolated.

---

### 3. Kael Orin — The Brassfire
**Background:** Combine infantry veteran who went independent after his regiment was sacrificed to cover a strategic retreat. Loud and physical, fights like he's trying to make a point. Commands infantry with a voice that carries across a battlefield. Has never met a fortified position he didn't want to walk through.

**Aura — Rally:** Nearby infantry ignore suppression penalties at half their normal threshold — they pin later and recover faster.

**Signature Ability — Charge:** Orin leads a direct assault — he and all infantry in his radius sprint forward ignoring suppression entirely for 6 seconds, dealing bonus melee damage on contact. Visually: red-orange motion blur, speed lines, every soldier behind him moving like a single wave.

**Tier progression:** More battered, more decorated. By Tier 4 he's covered in unit patches from every outfit he's ever fought alongside.

**Role:** Assault hero. Plant him in your infantry line and push. Straightforward and lethal.

---

### 4. Sable — The Foundling (Ironborn, joins Act 2)
**Background:** First robot confirmed self-aware. Led the Ironborn during the betrayal arc. Chose to return. Doesn't fully understand human grief but is learning. Visually massive — industrial chassis, improvised surface details that suggest personality rather than program.

**Aura — Iron Calm:** Nearby units (human and robot) are immune to the broken morale state — they can be shaken but will not rout while Sable is present.

**Signature Ability — Absolute Hold:** Sable plants and emits a suppression field — all enemy units in a wide radius are fully pinned for 10 seconds regardless of cover or morale. Sable cannot move during this. Visually: deep blue-white geometric light radiates outward, ancient units affected differently (they writhe, they resist, they sometimes break the hold early).

**Tier progression:** Improvised additions become more deliberate — Ironborn craft things onto Sable between missions. By Tier 4 they carry markings from every human soldier who's fought alongside them.

**Role:** Anchor hero. Hold a line, pin an assault, buy time. Irreplaceable in Act 3.

---

### 5. Yenna Crost — The Threadcutter
**Background:** Covenant intelligence operative who went dark after a deep-cover assignment inside Hollow-corrupted territory. Never supposed to be on a battlefield. Ended up there. Has an intuitive read on the Hollow's subsuming mode that nobody can explain — she thinks in their patterns without being consumed by them. Unnerving to be around. Indispensable.

**Aura — Exposed:** Enemy units near Crost have their armor facing revealed to all friendly units — flanking bonuses against them are increased, and their cover state is shown clearly on the UI.

**Signature Ability — Unravel:** Crost targets a single enemy unit or structure — it is marked and takes massively increased damage from all sources for 8 seconds. Visually: iridescent threads visibly connect her to the target, then pull taut and snap.

**Tier progression:** Increasingly difficult to look at directly — visual noise around her increases. By Tier 4 the UI briefly glitches when she uses her ability.

**Role:** Precision/assassination hero. Pair with your heaviest damage dealers. Destroys anything you point her at.

---

## Starting Choice
Players begin the campaign choosing between **Voss** and **Orin** — one sustain/mechanical, one assault/infantry. This shapes early playstyle and which units you lean on before the roster expands.

**Rel** is encountered and recruited through an Act 1 campaign event.
**Sable** joins at the Act 2 midpoint after the robot peace beat fires.
**Crost** is recruited through a choice in Act 2 — you can miss her if you don't pursue the Hollow intelligence thread.
